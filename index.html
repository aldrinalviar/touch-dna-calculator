Methodology & Equations

The two spreadsheet-equivalent calculators below reproduce the mathematical structure of the original Excel workbook. The Visual Transfer Pathway Builder is a separate sequential model and should not be assumed to be mathematically equivalent to the spreadsheet adjustment.

1. DNA Retention on Surface

Percent DNA recovery

Recovery fraction = Mean DNA recovered ÷ DNA deposited

Total DNA loss

Total loss fraction = 1 − Recovery fraction

Combined sampling loss

Sampling loss fraction = Swab Retention + DNA Extraction Loss

Estimated surface retention

Surface retention = Total loss − Combined sampling loss

2. Transfer Pathway Adjustment — Spreadsheet-Equivalent Model

Total modeled loss

Total loss (%) = Surface retention (%) + Swab retention (%) + Extraction loss (%)

Total modeled recovery

Total recovery (%) = 100 − Total loss (%)

DNA adjusted for loss

Adjusted DNA (ng) = Mean DNA recovered ÷ (Total recovery ÷ 100)

Adjusted amount relative to deposited DNA

Adjusted DNA (%) = (Adjusted DNA ÷ DNA deposited) × 100

3. Visual Transfer Pathway Builder — Sequential Model

The pathway builder applies each event to the DNA amount remaining after the previous event. It is therefore a sequential multiplicative model.

Surface retention or sampling loss

DNA after = DNA before × (1 − loss fraction)

Transfer or recovery event

DNA after = DNA before × event fraction

Because the spreadsheet adjustment adds loss percentages before back-calculating, while the pathway builder applies percentages sequentially, the two methods can produce different results from the same nominal percentages. They answer different modeling questions.

Visual Transfer Pathway Builder

Build a sequence of DNA transfer, recovery, surface-retention, or laboratory-loss events. Surface-retention values represent DNA that remains on the surface and is therefore treated as unavailable for downstream recovery. Swab retention and extraction loss are classified as sampling losses and are also subtracted from the amount entering that step. Transfer and recovery values multiply by the stated fraction. Use this as an exploratory model only.
